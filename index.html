PhD Physics, TIFR Hyderabad

I am an electrical engineer with a deep-rooted passion for the core principles of my field. During my undergraduate years, I was particularly drawn to electrical machines, generators, and transformers—systems that embody the beauty of classical electromagnetism and energy conversion.

Over time, my curiosity has expanded toward the more fundamental realms of science, especially light–matter interactions, the electronic and optical properties of optoelectronic materials, and spectroscopy. This evolving interest reflects my desire to bridge applied engineering with foundational science, aiming to understand not only how systems work, but why they behave the way they do.

Research

My PhD research focuses on the optical and electronic properties of organic semiconductors, perovskites and 2D materials, studied through spectroscopy, electrical characterization and charge transport. In organic electronics, I developed adduct-based dopants that suppress ion migration and enable high-performance optoelectronic devices. I also studied how the counter-anion sets the density of states, mobility, carrier density and polaron formation, which in turn govern charge localization and transport.

Research interests

Doping of organic semiconductors

Adduct-based p-type dopants that suppress ion migration and improve device performance.

Charge transport and polarons

How counter-anions shape mobility, carrier density and charge localization.

Light–matter spectroscopy

Optical and electronic properties of organic, perovskite and 2D materials.
